# Visual Quality Checklist

Use this as a review pass, not a checklist to satisfy mechanically. Fix causes,
not symptoms.

## First impression

- The subject is recognisable from the design, not only from the copy.
- One element clearly leads the composition.
- The first viewport is readable at 1280×720 without hiding the point below a
  decorative hero.
- The palette and type choices feel related to the subject.
- Nothing looks like it was left at a framework default.

## Hierarchy

- Headings, body, labels and metadata have distinct, consistent roles.
- The eye can move from primary to secondary to supporting information without
  guessing.
- Repeated sections vary in density or composition where the content changes.
- Actions are visually distinct from navigation and from passive information.
- Secondary information does not compete with primary information through
  equal weight, contrast or motion.

## Layout

- Alignment edges are intentional and repeated.
- Spacing follows a small scale rather than arbitrary values.
- Related elements are closer than unrelated elements.
- Cards are not nested without a structural reason.
- Wide content is constrained; dense content is grouped.
- Nothing clips, overlaps or shifts at 360px, 768px, 1280px or 200% zoom.
- Long words, long labels and real data do not break the layout.

## Typography

- Body measure is comfortable (roughly 55–75 characters).
- Heading line breaks are deliberate, not accidental.
- Font weights and sizes create hierarchy without making everything heavy.
- Numerals align where comparison matters (tables, prices, metrics).
- Buttons, labels and captions have consistent casing and spacing.
- There are no stray straight quotes, double spaces or orphaned punctuation in
  prominent copy.

## Colour and surfaces

- Text contrast meets WCAG AA at the rendered size.
- Focus indicators are visible against every surface they can appear on.
- Accent colour is used for emphasis and action, not for every border.
- Surface elevation communicates a real layer change.
- Gradients, glows and transparency support the visual idea rather than
  replacing it.
- Disabled states remain legible enough to understand what is unavailable.

## Interaction

- Every interactive element has hover, focus-visible, active and disabled states
  where relevant.
- Click/tap targets are at least 44×44px where practical.
- Destructive actions are clearly distinguished and require confirmation when
  the consequence is not reversible.
- Loading, empty, error and success states are designed.
- Keyboard order matches visual order; dialogs trap focus and restore it on
  close.
- Essential information is never available only on hover.

## Motion

- Motion explains a change in state, hierarchy or space.
- Repeated hover motion does not distract from reading.
- Reduced-motion users receive an equivalent, calm experience.
- Animations do not cause layout shift or block interaction.
- Nothing loops indefinitely beside body text unless it is ambient by design.

## Content and finish

- Placeholder copy has been replaced with realistic content.
- Icons share stroke weight and optical size.
- Images have alt text, correct aspect ratios and no distortion.
- Currency, date, unit and locale formatting are consistent.
- The interface still looks finished with the longest and shortest realistic
  content.
- The final screen was inspected at actual size, not only in a zoomed-out
  design view.
